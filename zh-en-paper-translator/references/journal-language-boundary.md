# Optional Journal-Language Boundary

The target journal is optional. The Chinese manuscript may already reflect the journal's structure and emphasis, so do not demand journal information merely to translate it.

## When No Journal Is Supplied

Use neutral international academic English. Select one spelling convention and apply it consistently. Do not guess a journal or imitate a prestigious journal's prose.

## When a Journal Is Supplied

Consult the journal's current official author instructions when access is available. Apply only explicit language-level requirements relevant to translation, such as:

- required British or American spelling;
- prescribed nomenclature, capitalization, abbreviations, symbols, or units;
- person-first, inclusive, sex/gender, species, gene/protein, or statistical language;
- required wording for standardized declarations or structured abstract labels.

Record the official source and the rule applied. If a rule cannot be verified, state that it was not applied rather than inferring it from published articles.

## Excluded Journal Adaptation

Do not use the journal option to:

- alter claims, evidence, novelty, or causal strength;
- reorganize paragraphs or sections;
- enforce word limits by deleting content;
- imitate favored phrases from a small sample of papers;
- add editorial framing, citations, declarations, or reporting content absent from the source;
- treat peer-reviewed examples as official house rules.

If the user later requests journal adaptation beyond language mechanics, treat that as a separate writing or polishing task requiring explicit authorization.
